Team offsite

Idea sprint

Ninety minutes, three rounds, one shortlist.

Facilitator: Team Harbor

Welcome aboard. Today we will generate many ideas quickly and pick a few worth testing. There are three short rounds, a break, and a vote. You do not need to be creative on command; the structure does the work. Please put phones away for the rounds, and keep the sticky notes within reach.

The plan

Four stops in ninety minutes

  1. 10 minWarm-up and rules
  2. 45 minThree idea rounds
  3. 15 minBreak and sort
  4. 20 minVote and next steps
Here is the whole day on one line. Ten minutes to warm up and agree on rules. Forty-five minutes for three idea rounds of fifteen minutes each. A short break while I cluster the notes on the wall. Then twenty minutes to vote and name an owner for each winner. I will keep time out loud.

The one rule

No idea gets judged until the round ends.

This is the only rule that really matters. Judging too early is the main reason groups produce few ideas. If you hear yourself say yes-but, say yes-and instead. Critique has its place, and it comes after the vote. I will gently stop anyone, including myself, who starts to evaluate during a round.

Round one · 15 min

Write fast, alone

  1. Read the question aloud
  2. Write one idea per note
  3. Stick it on the wall

Aim for ten notes each.

Round one is silent and solo. I will read the question once. You then write as many ideas as you can, one per note, big enough to read from two metres away. Quantity matters more than quality at this stage. After ten minutes we stick everything on the wall and take a minute to read each other's notes.

Next

Build on each other

Rounds two and three work in pairs.

Rounds two and three are different. You work in pairs, and you start from somebody else's note rather than your own. This is where most good ideas appear: a small idea combined with another small idea. Take a note from the wall that you did not write, and make it better, bigger or stranger.

In each pair

Two roles, swap halfway

Builder

Adds to the note.

Scribe

Writes the result.

Each pair has two roles. The builder speaks and extends the idea; the scribe writes the improved version on a new note. Swap roles after seven minutes so both of you do both jobs. If you run dry, take another note from the wall. A pair that stops to argue has stopped to judge, so go back to yes-and.

After the break

How we choose

  • Each person gets three dots
  • Place them silently, all at once
  • The top five go to the shortlist
After the break the notes are grouped by theme. Each person gets three dots. Place them silently and at the same time, so nobody follows the crowd. Spread them or stack them as you like. We count, and the top five clusters become the shortlist. If there is a tie for fifth place, we discuss for two minutes and then decide.

Targets

What good looks like

100notes on the wall
5shortlisted ideas
5named owners

Source: facilitator plan for a group of ten; scale the targets to your team size.

These are targets, not promises. For a group of ten, about a hundred notes is a healthy wall. Five shortlisted ideas is enough to test and few enough to finish. Every shortlisted idea needs one owner and a first step before we leave the room. Without an owner, a shortlist is only a nicer list.

Land the ideas

Owners, first steps and dates before we leave the room.

Thank you for the energy. Before you leave, each owner writes a first step and a date on the shortlist sheet by the door. I will photograph the wall and send the notes and the sheet to everyone tomorrow. In two weeks we meet for fifteen minutes to see which ideas moved. Please take your sticky notes home if you want them.
