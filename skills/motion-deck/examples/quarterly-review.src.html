Revenue grew 14% while late deliveries fell by a third.

If you remember only one sentence, take this one. We grew faster than planned and, for the first time in two years, we did it without paying for it in service quality. The next slides show the numbers behind each half of the sentence, and then the places where we did not do as well.

What went well

Three wins worth repeating

Route planning

New tool cut empty miles by 9%.

Hub handover

Night shift now ends clean.

Customer calls

Proactive delay alerts went live.

Three changes explain most of the improvement. The route planning tool removed nine percent of empty miles. The hub handover checklist ended the habit of leaving unsorted pallets for the morning team. Proactive delay alerts reduced inbound calls, so the support team could handle the volume growth without hiring. All three are ready to copy to other depots.

Part 2

What we missed

Three misses, in order of cost.

Now the less comfortable part. We missed three targets, and I have ordered them by what they cost us, not by how embarrassing they were. For each one I will say what happened, what we believe the cause is, and what we decided to do about it. I would rather list them here than have you find them later.

Misses

Where we fell short

  • Warehouse overtime ran 18% over budget
  • Two driver hires slipped by six weeks
  • Fuel savings stayed below target
Overtime is the costliest miss: the extra volume arrived faster than the hiring. That leads to the second miss, because two driver hires slipped by six weeks while we waited for licence checks. Fuel savings were the smallest of the three. We expected the route tool to deliver more, and we think the effect is delayed, not absent.

What we learned

Cause and change

Cause

We hired after the volume, not before it.

Change

Hiring now follows the signed pipeline.

The root cause behind the first two misses is the same: we started hiring when the volume arrived rather than when the contracts were signed. The change is simple and already agreed with finance. From this quarter, hiring requests follow the signed pipeline, with a lead time of eight weeks. This one rule would have avoided most of the overtime.
